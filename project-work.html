01 — Research design

The Role of Digital Market Research in Understanding Sustainable Fashion Choices Among UK Gen Z in the Digital Age

An MSc dissertation exploring how digital and psychological factors shape sustainable fashion purchase intention and recent behaviour.

Research design

Positivist philosophy with a quantitative, cross-sectional design. A 25-item online questionnaire used five-point Likert scales and targeted UK-based Gen Z respondents aged 18–27.

Online questionnaireUK Gen ZCross-sectional

Analysis

132 valid responses were analysed in SPSS v31 using descriptive statistics, Pearson correlations and multiple regression. Constructs included attitude, subjective norms, perceived behavioural control, brand authenticity, price sensitivity, social media influence and purchase intention.

SPSSRegressionCorrelation
R2 = 0.601

Purchase intention model

The model explains 60.1% of the variance in sustainable purchase intention. Attitude and subjective norms were the strongest positive predictors.

R2 = 0.513

Recent behaviour model

The model explains 51.3% of the variance in recent sustainable behaviour. Purchase intention was the strongest predictor of recent sustainable behaviour.

Ethics approval (MMU) was documented, with voluntary participation and GDPR compliance.

06 — Research methodology

When I Let AI Analyse My Dissertation Data

A comparison of SPSS regression with AI-assisted interpretation using the same sustainable-fashion research data from 132 UK Gen Z respondents.

SPSS identified the statistically significant drivers; AI surfaced an accessibility-belief loop, reliability concerns and additional questions for investigation. The conclusion is that the methods are complementary, with human judgement remaining essential.

07 — Anonymised agency experience

Why GPs were not prescribing a new antidepressant

Pharmaceutical client · 400 GPs · two-phase qualitative and quantitative study.

A pharmaceutical client wanted to understand why prescribing of a new antidepressant was below forecast. Qualitative interviews explored the barriers, followed by a quantitative survey with 400 GPs to size them.

When response rates fell below target during fieldwork, I worked with the client to raise the respondent incentive and open additional GP recruitment networks. The study finished on schedule and the sample held to quota.

The findings informed a change in the product go-to-market approach. Client and product details are withheld.
